What is already on this calendar

From last week to a year ahead. Tap one to edit it. A repeating event shows once, and editing it changes the whole series.

A message across the top of the TV in the foyer, above everything else. For "service moved to 6pm" — it appears within a couple of minutes, without waiting for the hourly sync.

Leaving the end date blank keeps it up until somebody takes it down. A date is safer: nothing looks worse on a wall than last month's urgent notice.

On the printed card

Standing notes printed at the foot of the calendar card, one per line. They exist so something on a regular rhythm is one sentence rather than eight identical dated lines eating the card.

Anything marked Routine is kept off the card entirely, so a note here is how people still hear about it. It appears on the next card generated, not on cards already printed.

Make the card

Builds a PDF of the next two months and emails it, ready to send to the printer. Takes a minute or two — the email arrives on its own, and you get one either way if something goes wrong.

Who has said they are coming. The contact on each event gets one email a day with the whole list, on days when something changed.

Who gets which calendars

People choose the public calendars themselves when they sign up. The private ones, shown with a dashed outline, can only be granted here. Changes take effect on their existing link at the next sync, so there is nothing for them to redo on their phone.

Who may open this page. Signing in checks the Google account against this list, so adding somebody here is what lets them in, and taking them off is what stops them — no passcode to change, and nobody else disturbed.
